Product Strategy & Architecture · V1.0

Ofia Commerce & Logistics Ecosystem

One platform. Six applications. Seven vertical experiences. One simple experience for every business.

Six applicationsMulti-vertical commerceIntegrated logistics

Product vision: Build a unified commerce and logistics ecosystem that gives every business a storefront and operating experience tailored to its industry, while giving customers one simple place to discover businesses, shop, book services, and arrange deliveries.

Core principle: A car dealer should feel like they are using a platform designed for car dealers. A food vendor should feel like they are using a platform designed for food businesses. Both operate through the same shared platform.

1. Product Foundation

1.1 Product promises

  • Industry-native experience: Each business gets a storefront and management interface suited to its industry.
  • Simple onboarding: Select a vertical, configure the store, and start selling.
  • Unified commerce: Products, services, bookings, orders, payments, and customer relationships work together.
  • Integrated logistics: Customers and vendors can independently arrange dispatch and pickup services.
  • Omnichannel access: Businesses manage operations through web and mobile applications.
  • Flexible growth: Vendors can enable additional capabilities as their businesses expand.

1.2 Six applications in V1

WEB 01

Ofia Storefront

Customer-facing business storefronts rendered through vertical-specific web templates.

WEB 02

Ofia Vendor Dashboard

Business management, store customization, products, services, orders, payments, and operations.

WEB 03

Ofia Logistics Agent Dashboard

Dispatch management, delivery coordination, rider assignment, and operational monitoring.

MOBILE 01

Ofia Customer App

Native shopping, vendor storefronts, service booking, order tracking, and independent logistics requests.

MOBILE 02

Ofia Vendor App

Mobile-first business management with vertical-specific product, service, order, and store customization interfaces.

MOBILE 03

Ofia Rider App

Rider onboarding, job assignments, pickup and delivery execution, navigation, and earnings.

1.3 Separate public website

Ofia.ng remains the public platform entry point: ecosystem overview, product information, business registration, vendor acquisition, app links, pricing, contact, and support forms. Ofia Compass and broader marketplace discovery can be developed later without making them a dependency for the initial storefront and commerce release.

Customer, vendor, rider, agent, staff accounts; roles, permissions, sessions, recovery. A user may hold multiple roles.

Business & stores

Registration, verification, store creation, vertical/template selection, URL assignment, branding, configuration, publishing.

Product catalog

Products, categories, images, pricing, variants, stock, availability, attributes, vertical metadata.

Services & bookings

Service listings, pricing, duration, availability, calendar, requests, confirmations, cancellations, provider assignment.

Commerce & orders

Cart, checkout, order creation/status, fulfillment, cancellation, refunds, customer history.

Payments & billing

Payment initiation/confirmation, transactions, settlements, refunds, platform fees, billing configuration.

Logistics & dispatch

Delivery jobs, pickup requests, rider assignment, pricing, tracking, proof of pickup/delivery, exceptions, reports.

Notifications

Push, in-app, email, SMS/OTP where needed; order, booking, dispatch, and payment alerts.

Customer relationships

Profiles, order history, inquiries, communication, saved stores, reviews/feedback where enabled.

10. Vertical Template Architecture

10.1 Template principle

Do not build seven independent applications. Each relevant app contains a vertical experience layer that selects the appropriate interface using the store's vertical and configuration.

Vendor creates store → Selects vertical → Chooses template → Configures brand/sections → Enables products/services/bookings → Publishes store

Published configuration feeds: Storefront Web Renderer · Customer Mobile Renderer · Vendor Web Workspace · Vendor Mobile Workspace

10.2 Store template configuration

  • Vertical identifier and template identifier
  • Theme settings and homepage section order
  • Enabled capabilities and catalog fields
  • Enabled service types
  • Branding and store policies
  • Feature visibility

10.3 Each template defines

  1. Storefront homepage layout
  2. Product/listing card design
  3. Product detail layout
  4. Service presentation
  5. Search/filter behavior
  6. Checkout or inquiry behavior
  7. Vendor catalog editor
  8. Vendor dashboard widgets
  9. Mobile-specific layouts
  10. Customization options

10.4 Shared vs. vertical-specific components

Shared foundationsVertical-specific components
Header/navigation, product-card foundation, image gallery, search/filter engine, cart/checkout, payment status, booking engine, order managementVehicle specification panel, food menu item, property listing, apparel variant selector, gadget comparison, beauty service menu, furniture dimensions

Build shared foundations once, then compose vertical experiences from them.

11. End-to-End User Journeys

11.1 Vendor onboarding

  1. Visit Ofia.ng and select business registration.
  2. Create account and enter business information.
  3. Select one of seven verticals and a storefront template.
  4. Configure brand identity and add products/services.
  5. Configure delivery and payment options.
  6. Preview and publish the store.
  7. Manage operations through web or mobile.

11.2 Customer shopping

  1. Open Customer App and search/discover a vendor.
  2. Open storefront; app renders the correct vertical template.
  3. Browse products/services; add to cart or initiate booking.
  4. Complete checkout/payment and receive confirmation.
  5. Track order or booking and receive updates.

11.3 Independent logistics

  1. Open Logistics and select pickup request.
  2. Enter pickup/destination and parcel details.
  3. Receive quote, confirm, and pay as configured.
  4. Operator receives job and assigns rider.
  5. Customer tracks pickup/delivery; delivery is confirmed.

11.4 Vendor dispatch

  1. Open vendor app/dashboard and choose Book Dispatch or Request Pickup.
  2. Enter locations and parcel details; confirm request.
  3. Operator receives job and assigns rider.
  4. Vendor tracks progress until completion.

12. Product Administration & Operations

Internal operational controls can initially live in a protected admin interface rather than becoming another customer-facing application.

  • Business/vendor management and verification
  • Store approval, suspension, and template configuration/publishing
  • User and role management
  • Logistics agent management and rider verification
  • Payment monitoring, reconciliation, refunds, disputes
  • Support ticket management
  • Platform configuration and audit logs

13. Non-Functional Requirements

Performance

  • Fast storefront loading and optimized images
  • Lazy-loaded galleries and responsive layouts
  • Efficient mobile data usage and paginated catalogs

Reliability

  • Reliable order creation and payment reconciliation
  • Duplicate-payment protection
  • Consistent order/delivery status
  • Retry handling for failed notifications and audit trails

Security

  • Role-based authorization and business data isolation
  • Secure payment integration
  • Protected vendor/rider endpoints
  • Account verification and sensitive-data protection

Mobile usability

  • Touch-friendly interfaces, push notifications, clear loading/error states
  • Network interruption handling
  • Location permissions for logistics
  • Camera/photo uploads for products and delivery proof

Extensibility

  • Additional verticals, product attributes, service capabilities, payment methods, logistics providers, storefront sections, and vendor roles

14. V1 Delivery Roadmap

Phase 1 — Platform foundation

Identity/business onboarding; store creation and vertical selection; store configuration; shared catalog; service/booking capability; core order/payment workflows; initial logistics job model.

Phase 2 — Commerce experience

Storefront web, customer mobile, vendor web/mobile; first vertical templates; product browsing and checkout; store customization and publishing.

Phase 3 — Logistics experience

Agent dashboard and rider app; store-order delivery; independent customer pickup; vendor dispatch/pickup; assignment, tracking, delivery confirmation.

Phase 4 — Complete V1 vertical coverage

Fashion, Cars, Food, Property Rentals, Gadgets, Beauty, Home & Living; vertical-specific vendor management; service and booking enhancements.

Phase 5 — Optimization and growth

Advanced analytics, reviews, following/loyalty, promotions, logistics automation, and expanded storefront customization.

15. V1 Acceptance Criteria

  • A vendor can register and create a store.
  • A vendor can select one of the seven vertical templates.
  • Storefront web renders the selected vertical correctly.
  • Customer mobile renders the same store natively, without a web wrapper.
  • Vendor manages products/services from web and mobile.
  • Customization is reflected consistently across customer interfaces.
  • Customers browse, purchase, and track orders.
  • Customers book enabled services.
  • Customers create independent pickup requests.
  • Vendors create dispatch and pickup requests independently.
  • Logistics dashboard receives and assigns jobs.
  • Riders accept, execute, and complete jobs.
  • Customers/vendors receive relevant notifications.
  • Payment and order statuses remain consistent across apps.
  • Multiple businesses operate independently.

16. Final Product Design Principles

  1. One business, one coherent experience. Avoid disconnected tools.
  2. Vertical-specific interfaces, shared foundations. Tailor experiences without duplicating the platform.
  3. Native mobile rendering. Customer app uses coded mobile templates, not embedded web pages.
  4. Consistent vendor operations. Web and mobile share configuration, data, and workflows.
  5. Commerce first, services where relevant. Products are central; services, appointments, and requests are flexible capabilities.
  6. Logistics is independent. Dispatch can be requested without a store purchase.
  7. Simple by default. Show businesses the controls they need, not every platform feature.
  8. Progressive capability. Vendors enable features as they grow.
  9. Brand flexibility. Each storefront should feel like the vendor's own digital shop.
  10. Build for extension. Add verticals through reusable templates and configuration rather than extensive rewrites.
Defining competitive experience: Ofia does not merely give every business an online store. It gives every business an online store and management experience that feels familiar to the way that business operates.
