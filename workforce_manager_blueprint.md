# 1. Executive vision

I think this is a product worth building, particularly because we have an immediate internal use case.

However, I want us to define it correctly from the beginning.

We are not building another HR application.

We are building a platform that helps organisations find people, recruit them, develop their skills, organise their work, manage their availability, deploy them to business operations and customers, and measure the value they deliver.

Think about the entire lifecycle:

## The Workforce Lifecycle

1. Recruit

   Advertise vacancies, receive applications, screen candidates and conduct interviews.
2. Onboard

   Convert successful applicants into interns, employees or contractors.
3. Develop

   Train people, assign learning materials, conduct examinations and certify competencies.
4. Manage

   Assign tasks, manage schedules, track attendance and evaluate performance.
5. Deploy

   Allocate qualified people to departments, projects, client accounts and service contracts.
6. Measure

   Monitor productivity, service quality, utilisation, operational costs and revenue contribution.
7. Progress

   Promote, specialise, retrain or transition people into new responsibilities.

The distinction is important.

Traditional HR software primarily manages the employment relationship. Workforce management software also manages how an organisation's human resources are actually used to deliver work.

For example:

- A school can recruit teachers, train them and allocate them to classes.
- An IT company can recruit technicians, certify their skills and deploy them to customer contracts.
- A logistics company can recruit riders, manage their availability and allocate them to dispatch operations.
- A software company can recruit customer success agents and assign them to client accounts.
- A compliance firm can recruit analysts and allocate supervised compliance cases to them.

This gives us a much broader market than conventional HR software.

# 2. Product positioning and identity

I would position this as a standalone product in the Ofia Enterprise ecosystem.

Working name: WorkforceOS

The name is provisional and should undergo trademark and domain checks before adoption.

Proposed positioning:

The operating system for managing people, skills, work and workforce deployment.

The product should serve four major markets:

| Market                   | What they need                                                         |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| SMEs                     | Recruitment, staff records, attendance and tasks                       |
| Growing companies        | Training, performance and workforce planning                           |
| Outsourcing companies    | Staff allocation, client assignments and service delivery              |
| Enterprise organisations | Multi-department, multi-location and multi-client workforce management |

The product should be modular, so a small business doesn't have to purchase the entire system.

### Example deployment workflow

An IT outsourcing company receives a contract to provide technical support to a bank.

The operations manager creates a workforce request:

```
Client: ABC Bank
Contract: IT Support Services
Required personnel: 5
Required skills:
- Networking
- Windows administration
- Helpdesk
- IT documentation

Duration: 12 months
Location: Lagos
Supervisor: IT Operations Manager
```

WorkforceOS searches its people directory and identifies candidates based on:

- Skills.
- Competency levels.
- Certifications.
- Availability.
- Location.
- Existing assignments.
- Experience.
- Employment status.

The operations manager selects suitable personnel.

The deployment then goes through approval.

This is fundamentally different from a conventional HR employee directory.

- Compensation records.
- Stipend management.

## J. Client portal

For outsourcing businesses, clients need limited visibility into the services being delivered.

Their portal should display:

- Assigned personnel.
- Service requests.
- Support tickets.
- Progress reports.
- SLA performance.
- Work completion.
- Escalations.
- Approved timesheets.
- Customer feedback.

Clients should never have access to internal HR records, private employee information or other customers' accounts.

## C. Critical identity decision

Do not make a candidate, intern and employee three unrelated entities.

Use a permanent `people` record.

A candidate becomes an intern through a status and relationship change. An intern can subsequently become an employee.

The same person can also have different engagements over time.

For example:

- November: Design intern.
- January: Design associate.
- April: Full-time employee.
- August: Client deployment.
- December: Design team lead.

All historical records should remain accessible.

For contractors working for multiple client engagements, separate the person's employment relationship from their individual deployments.

## D. Multi-tenancy

I recommend a shared database with organisation-level tenant isolation initially.

Every tenant-owned record should carry an `organisation_id`.

All queries must enforce tenant boundaries.

For particularly sensitive modules, add PostgreSQL Row-Level Security as an additional protection.

The system should support:

- Multiple companies under one group.
- Multiple branches.
- Multiple departments.
- Different subscription plans.
- Custom roles.
- Tenant-specific branding.
- Tenant-specific workflows.
- Tenant-specific module activation.

This means one WorkforceOS installation can serve hundreds or thousands of organisations.

This is where the product becomes strategically interesting.

### Authentication and integration

Use shared authentication through the Ofia identity layer, preferably OAuth 2.0 / OpenID Connect.

Each application should have:

- Its own module access.
- Its own domain routes.

The integration layer should use:

- REST APIs.
- Webhooks.
- Shared identity references.
- External resource IDs.
- Event-based updates where appropriate.

For example, when an opportunity becomes a signed service engagement in CRM, a corresponding workforce request can be created in WorkforceOS.

That does not mean the CRM and WorkforceOS should share every database table.

They should communicate through defined contracts.

# 9. Commercial model and pricing strategy

Now to the commercial opportunity.

The biggest mistake would be to price this exactly like a traditional HR application.

We should distinguish between people whose records are managed and people who actively use the platform.

For example:

A company might have 200 field workers but only 15 managers, supervisors and administrators.

Charging the same full licence price for all 200 may make adoption difficult.

I recommend a hybrid model.

## A. Three licensing categories

| Licence          | Who uses it                           | Pricing approach             |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Administrator    | HR, management and operations         | Paid seat                    |
| Professional     | Supervisors, instructors and managers | Paid seat or role-based tier |
| Workforce member | Employees, interns and field workers  | Low-cost or included access  |

This allows the platform to scale without making every employee an expensive software seat.

For example, a company with 100 staff could have:

- 3 administrators.
- 12 supervisors.
- 85 workforce members.

The 85 employees may need only attendance, tasks, learning and personal records.

They should not have to pay for full administrative functionality.

### Development cost-control strategy

- Reuse your existing authentication infrastructure.
- Reuse your established frontend and backend conventions.
- Reuse existing CRM integrations.
- Integrate CoursesPro where appropriate instead of duplicating an entire LMS.
- Use standard workflow templates.
- Avoid native mobile applications until the responsive web experience is validated.
- Build modules incrementally.
- Avoid microservices until actual scale requires them.

One particularly important decision: WorkforceOS should own competency records and workforce deployment, while CoursesPro can provide the actual learning delivery engine.

This avoids building two competing learning management systems.

We can initially embed or integrate the CoursesPro learning experience while keeping enrolment, competencies and workforce progression connected to WorkforceOS.

# 23. My final recommendation

I believe this is a worthwhile addition to the Ofia Enterprise ecosystem.

But I would be very deliberate about the market we enter.

There are already HR platforms in Nigeria. We will not win simply by offering employee records, leave management and attendance at a lower price.

Our differentiation should be the connection between people development and operational delivery.

A company should be able to:

1. Find people.
2. Recruit them.
3. Train them.
4. Establish what they are qualified to do.
5. Assign them to work.
6. Deploy them to customers.
7. Measure their performance.
8. Understand the cost and revenue of their deployment.

That is the product.

And there is an important business opportunity beyond selling software: we can use this same platform to build our own trained workforce and sell implementation, technical support, tutoring, compliance administration and customer success services.

My suggested development order is: Recruitment → People → Academy → Tasks → Performance → Workforce Deployment → Service Delivery.

Do not start with payroll. Do not rebuild CRM. Do not build a massive ERP. Do not start with mobile apps.

Build the talent-to-deployment engine first.

That gives us an immediately useful internal system, a foundation for IT outsourcing, and eventually a commercially independent product that can be sold to other Nigerian businesses.

The first milestone should be simple: within the first release, someone should be able to discover an opportunity, apply, get interviewed, receive an offer, become an intern, complete training, submit work, receive an evaluation and enter the workforce directory without anyone manually recreating their profile.

If we achieve that cleanly, we will have built the foundation of something much bigger.
